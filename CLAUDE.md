# ShawnAndersonWebSite

Shawn Anderson's personal site, shawnandersonapps.com, plain HTML on GitHub Pages (branch `main`, repo root = site root). No build step, no framework. Three live sub-sites are actively maintained:

- `rick/` Rick Saunders' Western Wildlands Route tracker (Canada to Mexico by bike, Sept 6 to Nov 7, 2026). Updated most days.
- `danceflowers/` Dance Flowers, Leah's corsage, boutonnière, and bouquet business for school dances and weddings in Idaho Falls. Landing page plus order form. See "danceflowers/ page" below.

- `dough/` Dough, Shawn's pizza dough calculator with saved bakes and reports, behind logins. See "dough/ app" at the bottom.

Everything else at the root (`index.html`, etc.) is the personal landing site.

TOTAL_PLAN is now about 2,527. `gain` is the official Bikepacking Roots climb. `days`, `start`, `end` are Rick's plan. `TOTAL_PLAN` is computed from SEGMENTS; never hardcode 2,724 in copy, read TOTAL_PLAN.
- `LOG` one row per calendar day: `{day, date, end, miles, gain, loss, lat?, lng?, notes, updates?, course?, done?}`.
  - `updates: [{time:"10:30 am", miles:17, gain:370, text:"..."}]` oldest first; multiple check-ins in one day, shown grouped under the day.
  - `done:false` marks today while he is still riding. Row shows "Riding now", is excluded from completed-day math. To close the day remove `done:false` and fill in end, miles, gain, loss, notes.
  - `high`, `low` (feet) the day's high and low point from the onX ride card, when known. Optional; the records page uses them for "Highest point reached" and "Lowest point reached". Add them when closing a day if the card shows them.
  - `wx: {hi, lo, rain, gust}` optional manual weather override for the records page (°F, °F, inches, mph). Normally absent; Open-Meteo fills it at load.
  - `course: N` how much a reroute that day changed the length of his course. Negative = shortcut (miles saved), positive = detour (miles added). See "Plan model" below.
- `LOCATION = {lat, lng, town, state, label, asOf, approx}` his last known position. `approx:false` means from his Garmin. Drives the "Where is Rick" block, the map, and the weather.
- `STATUS` one paragraph of intraday news shown under the position. Rewrite each update; `""` if nothing to say.
- `PROGRESS_MAP = {file, asOf, caption}` the zoomed-out map of the whole West with his track, shown under the route bar. Shawn sends a new screenshot every few days; overwrite `rick/photos/wwr-progress-map.jpg` (max 1600px) and bump `asOf`.

### Plan model (Shawn's decision, Sept 9, 2026; he is Rick's "race engineer")
- THE PLAN IS FIXED against Rick's own choices. Never edit `SEGMENTS.miles` because Rick rerouted.
- Exception (Shawn, Sept 14, 2026): a closure that removes the road (fire, washout) is a plan revision, not a reroute. Change the segment's `miles` and `gain` to the bypass route, re-time it at its original mi/day (`days` = round(new miles / original mi/day), new `end`), shift every later segment's `start`/`end` earlier by the days saved, and add `revised:{on, miles, gain, days, end, why}` with the originals. Do not add a `course` for it. Segment 2 on Sept 14: 320.5 mi / 8 days became 123.4 mi / 3 days (Sept 13 to 15), later segments moved up 5 days, plan finish Nov 2. Shawn's first cut kept the old dates and Needed pace showed 6 mi/day, which he rejected. Sept 18, 2026: Shawn also treated Rick's own reroute as a revision (not a shortcut): segment 3 became Darby to Mackay, 202.7 mi / 12,349 ft / 4 days (Sept 16 to 19), skipping Stanley, the Sawtooths and Hailey so Rick could go home to Idaho Falls for the weekend. Later segments moved up 3 more days. Shawn decides case by case which rule applies; ask when it is unclear.
- Reroutes are recorded on that day's LOG row as `course:N`. Shawn computes N in onX: he keeps a cloned route "Segment N with Shortcuts" mirroring what Rick actually rode plus the remaining plan; N = that route's total minus the original segment total. Day 3: 296.6 original, 289.8 with the shortcut, so `course:-6.8`. A detour (for example into Idaho Falls to see family) would be positive.
- Position = miles ridden minus sum of `course`. Versus plan, route bar, chart, segment status, remaining miles, projected finish and needed pace all use position. "Miles ridden" always shows real pedaled miles with a note about miles saved or added.
- Rick's onX plan is the target he committed to; a shortcut is a gain against it and a detour a cost.

### How the calculator works
- Modeled on Dough Guy's calculator (doughguy.co/pages/dough), which Shawn was using. His fixed recipe is the starting preset (`PRESET`): 62% water, 0.4% active dry yeast, 2.5% salt, 2% sugar, 3.3% olive oil, all as a percent of flour.
- Thickness is grams of dough per square inch. `THICK` stops match his thin, regular, thick (2.04, 2.39, 3.11; that is 409, 480, 626 g for a 16 inch pizza). Ball weight = thickness x area; flour = total dough / (1 + sum of percents).
- Unlike his, every ratio has a slider, thickness is a slider, and there are two more inputs: how long until baking (`FERMENT`: Quick about 2 hours, Same day 4 to 6 hours, Overnight 1 day in the fridge, 2 to 4 days; renamed Oct 3, 2026 after Shawn found "Tonight" and "Today" confusing, since tonight was the faster one) and yeast type (`YEAST`: active dry, instant x0.75, fresh x2.5). Picking a ferment plan sets a starting yeast percent and changes the steps. The yeast numbers for the shorter plans are Claude's rule-of-thumb starting points, not tested by Shawn; adjust them from his reports.
- Active dry yeast in cold water (Shawn's question, Oct 3, 2026): Dough Guy's steps stir it straight into cold water, but the yeast package says warm. The cold plans now bloom it in about 60 g of the water warmed to 105°F and keep the rest cold. Same-day plans use all warm water.
- "Lock it in" inserts a row in `dough_bakes` (the plan snapshot). "How did it go?" adds a 1 to 5 rating, went well, went poorly, and "next time, try". The newest bake and the newest "next time" note show in a banner above the calculator. "Make it again" loads a bake back into the calculator.
- Flour is a dropdown (Shawn, Oct 3, 2026: no retyping). `renderFlours()` builds it from the distinct `flour_name` values in that person's bakes, newest first, plus "Add a new flour…" which reveals the text box. With no bakes yet, only the text box shows. There is no separate flours table.
- Calculator state and the flour name are remembered in localStorage as a convenience only.

### Categories and recipes (Oct 4, 2026)
- Shawn's restructure after four "types" crowded the top: fixed CATEGORIES that Shawn and Claude decide, with each person's RECIPES inside. Categories are `CATS` in the page: Pizza Dough (`pizza`), Bread (`bread`), Rolls (`rolls`), Cinnamon Rolls (`cinnamon_rolls`), each with a usual sizing. Adding a category is a one-line change to `CATS`; ask Shawn first.
- A recipe is a row in `dough_types` (the table name and the word "type" in the code predate this; in everything the person reads it is "recipe"). `category` column added in `supabase/dough/004_categories.sql`, also on `dough_bakes` as a snapshot. Existing rows were sorted by name (cinnamon, pizza, roll or bun, else bread).
- The picker card at the top of the calculator: a row of category tabs with a recipe count, then a Recipe dropdown for that category with Edit, New recipe, and Ask AI. Each category remembers the recipe last opened in it (`lastIn`). A category with no recipes hides the calculator and shows "Ask AI for a starting point" or "Start one from scratch". Under 620px the category tabs are a two by two grid.
- The Edit dialog has Category and "Batch size by", and Delete is always available. An AI proposal saved as a new recipe goes in the category guessed from its name, falling back to the category on screen. My bakes filters by category.

### Dough types and steps (Oct 3, 2026)
- `supabase/dough/002_types_and_steps.sql`. Table `dough_types`, one row per person per type: `name`, `sizing`, `ingredients` (`[{key, name, pct}]`, flour is always 100 and not listed), `steps` (text, one per line, null = none written), `defaults` (the plan from the last locked-in bake). Everyone gets a "Pizza" type on first sign-in (`loadTypes()` inserts it and attaches older bakes to it). Signed out, the page uses an in-memory Pizza type only.
- `sizing` is how a batch is sized: `pizza` (count, diameter, thickness), `pieces` (count and grams each, for loaves and rolls), `flour` (start from the flour weight). The type bar above the calculator switches types; "New type" and "Edit <name>" open the type dialog (name, sizing, ingredient rows). New types start with Water, Yeast, Salt rows and EMPTY percents on purpose: never prefill a recipe Shawn did not give.
- Ingredient rows in the type dialog have up and down arrows (Shawn, Oct 3, 2026, wanted water at the top). The saved order is the order of the sliders and the weigh list.
- An ingredient whose name contains "yeast" gets key `yeast`, which turns on the yeast type switch. Water, salt, sugar, and oil keys get tuned slider ranges (`RANGES`); other ingredients get a range from their own percent.
- Steps belong to the person, not the app (Shawn, Oct 3, 2026: "not sure we want to get in the business of giving people steps"; his own are a mix of Dough Guy and Leo Spizzirri). The Steps card has "Edit steps" (a textarea, one per line). The generated basic steps only show for a pizza-sized type whose steps have never been edited, labeled as a starting point.
- Lock it in saves a snapshot on the bake (`type_id`, `type_name`, `sizing`, `ingredients`, `steps`) and writes the ratios, steps, and plan back to the type, so next time starts from the last bake. The old `pct` column is only on bakes from before this change. The yeast starting-point hint and the auto yeast change on ferment plan apply to pizza sizing only.
- Plan versus actual (Shawn, Oct 3, 2026: nobody should chase sliders to hit a gram): every number in the weigh list is a field. Typing the flour (`s.flour_fix`) re-scales the whole batch to that flour. Typing any other ingredient (`s.actual[key]`) records what went in and shows its true percent. Changing the plan sliders clears typed numbers (`replan()`). The bake saves the true percent in `pct` with the planned one in `plan_pct`; the type keeps the planned ratios, so an overpour does not become the recipe.
- Timing drives the steps (Shawn, Oct 4, 2026: timing is the hardest part for him, and he wanted "How long until you bake?" to change his steps). Each timing keeps its own version in `dough_types.variants` (`{ferment_key: {steps, yeast, yeast_type}}`). `setFerment()` saves the version being left and loads the one being entered. If the new timing has no version and the person has their own steps, a box under the steps says they were written for the other timing and offers "Adapt them for <timing>". That calls Edge Function `dough-adapt` (`supabase/dough/functions/dough-adapt/index.ts`, same access list, cap, and usage log as `dough-ai`, no web search), which returns rewritten steps, a yeast percent, and what changed. The person sees the proposal and picks "Use these" (saved to the type right away) or "No thanks". "Keep them as they are" makes the current steps that timing's version. The built-in Pizza basic steps still adapt on their own. Next step Shawn agreed to but not built: a clock schedule worked backward from when he wants to eat.
- My bakes shows type filter pills once there are two or more types. The "Last time" banner follows the type on screen.

### Ask AI (Oct 3, 2026)
- Shawn's idea: chat with AI to fill in a dough type, telling it who to look to for inspiration (ChainBaker, Leo Spizzirri), then edit before saving. Later it should read his reports and suggest the next change.
- Edge Function `dough-ai` in the ShawnZapps project; source in `supabase/dough/functions/dough-ai/index.ts` (deploy with the Supabase tools, `verify_jwt` on). It calls Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`, effort low, web search with `max_uses` 4) through the Anthropic SDK. Secret `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` was set by Shawn in Edge Functions, Secrets (its own key, created for this project). Never put it in the repo or the page.
- The model replies in plain text and, when it has a formula, calls the `propose_dough` tool (strict schema: summary, name, sizing, count, piece_g, flour_g, yeast_type, ingredients, steps, sources). The function returns `{reply, proposal, remaining}`. The page sends the chat as text plus `aiContext()` (current type, plan, ratios, steps, the last four bakes of that type with their reports).
- On the page: "Ask AI" pill in the type bar and a link in the New type dialog, both only when the person has access. The proposal card offers "Save as a new type" (inserts a `dough_types` row) or "Use in <current type>" (loads ratios and steps into the calculator, not saved until Lock it in). The chat lives in memory only and is gone on reload.
- Access and cost control, `supabase/dough/003_ai.sql`: `dough_ai_access` (one row per allowed user, `daily_limit` default 40 calls per 24 hours) and `dough_ai_usage` (one row per call with token counts). Signups are open, so the helper is allow-list only; Shawn's account is the only one on it. To add a friend: `insert into dough_ai_access (user_id) select id from auth.users where email = '...'`.
- The prompt tells it to write steps in its own words, name its sources, and say what is the source's number versus its own judgment.
